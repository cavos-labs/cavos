**Wallet-as-a-service (WaaS) is an API or SDK that provisions a crypto wallet inside your app in 2026.** The user signs in the way they already do. The platform looks up or creates an on-chain account for that login. Transactions are approved in your UI. There is no browser extension to install and no seed phrase to copy. That UX is an [embedded wallet](/embedded-wallet).

**Wallet as a service**, **WaaS**, and **embedded wallet as a service** are the same commercial label, named from how the wallet is sold. The full product and SDK page is [embedded wallet](/embedded-wallet). **Wallet as a service providers** are the vendors that sell it.

The label does not decide custody. Before you compare vendors, answer three questions: who provisions the wallet, who can produce a signature, and what the invoice meters. This guide is the 2026 definition, a dated shortlist (Privy, Dynamic, Turnkey, Dfns, Web3Auth, Para, Cavos), and the pricing-unit map. Replacement shortlists: [Privy alternatives](/privy-alternatives) and [Turnkey alternatives](/turnkey-alternatives). Cavos fees are on [pricing](/pricing).

Cavos, LLC is a software and SaaS company. The wallet is the main product: a hardware-isolated, non-custodial embedded wallet. Signing keys are created and used on the user’s device. Keys never leave the device in plaintext. Signing always happens on the device. Cavos cannot see them, sign with them, decrypt them, or move funds.

## What is wallet as a service?

**Wallet as a service** in 2026 is the unhyphenated form of **wallet-as-a-service** and **WaaS**. Teams type all three, plus embedded wallets as a service. The product is an API or SDK that turns a login into an on-chain account inside your app: authenticate the user, look up or create an address, sign from a key the platform manages or from a key on the device, and often sponsor the first fee. You do not ship a seed phrase or send the user to MetaMask.

A **wallet as a service** purchase usually includes some combination of: hosted or JWT login, a wallet registry keyed on that identity, key handling (device, MPC, enclave, or HSM), a signing API or `execute` method, gas sponsorship, recovery, and a dashboard for App IDs and spend limits. You are buying that path as software, not installing a browser extension.

That is how **wallet as a service** is sold. It is not a custody claim. Device-native keys, MPC shares, a remote enclave, and a delegated HSM are all sold under the same label. Write down who can produce a signature before you compare prices. WaaS vs an [embedded wallet](/embedded-wallet) is naming, not architecture: embedded is where the wallet appears; WaaS is how you buy it. The named-vendor table is [embedded wallet providers](/blog/embedded-wallet-providers).

## What is wallet-as-a-service?

A wallet-as-a-service, or an **embedded wallet platform**, turns a login into an on-chain account. You call an SDK or an API. The platform authenticates the user, looks up or creates an address, signs from a key it manages or from a key on the device, and often sponsors the first fee. You do not ship a seed phrase or send the user to MetaMask.

That is **where the wallet appears** and **how it is sold**. It is not a custody claim. The same dashboard can sit in front of device-native keys, MPC shares, a remote enclave, or a delegated HSM. Turnkey is closer to signing infrastructure. Privy, Dynamic, Magic, Web3Auth, and Cavos ship a finished login-to-signature path. Ask who can produce a valid signature without the user present. The [custody](/custody) page is the control test.

Three product shapes cover almost every vendor in the category:

- **Finished [embedded wallet SDK](/embedded-wallet).** Auth modal, wallet handle, sponsored `execute`, recovery. You integrate a kit. Cavos, Privy, Dynamic, Magic, and Web3Auth sit here, with different key models and chain lists.
- **Signing and key-management infrastructure.** Keys, policies, quorums, an HTTP or RPC surface. You assemble login and approval. Turnkey is the usual example. Privy vs Turnkey is usually [build versus buy](/blog/privy-vs-turnkey).
- **Connector plus embedded fallback.** The product also connects wallets the user already holds. Dynamic and Privy do this. Cavos does not. If existing extensions are a requirement, a connector is the better pick, and the two models can coexist.

Teams land here when the audience is not crypto-native. The tradeoff is that the application now owns recovery, device changes, gas, and how honest it is about who can sign.

## How wallet-as-a-service works: the moving parts

Write the answers down before you read a pricing page. Every WaaS product is some combination of the six parts below.

### Auth and login

Authentication exists for one reason: a stable user id the wallet registry can key on. **Login should select which user this is. It should not, by itself, authorize a spend.**

Hosted login is usually Google, Apple, email, SMS, or a passkey. Some vendors accept a JWT or a user id from your own auth. If email magic-link is enough to move funds, you have chosen a hosted model.

Cavos hosted login is Google, Apple, or email. If you already authenticate users, pass a stable `userId` as `identity` and Cavos login turns off. Use an immutable primary key, not an email that can change. See the [authentication docs](https://docs.cavos.xyz/docs/auth).

### Key management and custody

This is the part that decides whether the platform is a custodian in practice. Four designs cover the category:

- **Device-native.** The account key is derived and used on the user’s device. Keys never leave the device in plaintext. Signing always happens on the device. The provider cannot see the key, sign with it, or decrypt it. Cavos is this model. There is no MPC and no hosted spend key.
- **MPC / TSS.** Shares live on the device and on a provider network. Signing reconstructs or jointly computes a signature. Web3Auth describes threshold signatures and Shamir sharing. Dynamic describes TSS-MPC with a user share on the device and a server share in a TEE.
- **Remote enclave.** The key decrypts only inside a hardware-backed environment the vendor operates. Turnkey persists ciphertext and decrypts in AWS Nitro Enclaves. Custody is who holds the credentials that authorize the enclave.
- **Delegated TEE or HSM.** The provider’s key-management system holds or unwraps the key. Privy documents TEE key infrastructure. Magic’s public docs describe a TEE key-management system; the compare page also describes delegated HSMs.

“Non-custodial” is a claim. The test is who can move the assets. Privy ships non-custodial [embedded wallets](/embedded-wallet) and, separately, optional custodial wallets starting with Bridge. Counsel has to read recovery and export.

On Starknet, Cavos wallets are an **audited OpenZeppelin account contract** (AccountUpgradeable v3.0.0) controlled by one stark key derived from the MasterDEK and app salt. The audit covers OpenZeppelin’s contract, not Cavos. Cavos does not ship its own account implementation. There are no on-chain device signers.

### Signing

The device or the enclave produces a signature. Approval should happen in the product UI. A finished SDK exposes `execute` and `signMessage`. A signing API exposes keys, policies, and an HTTP surface; you build the approval screen.

Relayers and paymasters transport, sponsor, or submit. **They are not signers.** If the vendor can complete a transaction without the user, the platform is not self-custodial.

Cavos signs on the device. `Cavos.connect` looks up `(userId, appId, chain, network)` and returns the address; it never deploys Starknet or Stellar. The first `execute` deploys and runs atomically when the account is still undeployed. The [API guide](/blog/embedded-wallet-api) is the method map.

### Gas Sponsorship in Any Token

Users should not need a native token to start. Sponsorship is chain-specific, and it is not always on the public plan.

- **Starknet:** a paymaster sponsors deploy and execute.
- **Solana:** a relayer co-signs as fee payer. Arbitrary programs are allowlisted per app.
- **Stellar:** a relayer can sponsor reserves and fee-bump submission.
- **EVM smart accounts:** paymasters and bundlers. Dynamic lists gasless transactions on EVM and Solana as an Enterprise feature. Turnkey lists gas sponsorship on the Enterprise plan.

Ask whether an empty native-token balance can still send, and how the sponsorship credential is scoped. Cavos offers Gas Sponsorship in Any Token the user already holds (USDC is one example), funded separately from the wallet-create plans. A Starknet `paymasterApiKey` is a client-visible, app-scoped credential. Bind it by app, environment, origin, rate, and spend.

### Recovery

A device that is lost, wiped, or replaced takes its key with it. Recovery is a second authority set up *before* that happens.

In a WaaS product, recovery is usually a synced passkey, a cloud backup, a provider-held share, an enclave unwrap, or a recovery code. Those are not equivalent. A provider that cannot sign in the happy path but can restore a spend key in an incident is a different fact pattern from one that never can.

Cavos recovery is **off-chain**. A new device restores the MasterDEK with another signed-in device, a passkey, or opt-in hardware-isolated recovery in an AWS Nitro Enclave. The passkey and the enclave only hold an encrypted copy of the MasterDEK for sealed restore; they cannot sign, and Cavos cannot decrypt that copy. There is no on-chain social recovery and no on-chain device-signer list. If every device and every restore factor is gone, Cavos cannot recover the wallet. See [recovery](https://docs.cavos.xyz/docs/recovery).

### Dashboard and app management

The dashboard is how a platform becomes software you operate: App IDs, environments, API keys, origin and rate limits, sponsorship balances, allowlists, and usage. The invoice unit (MAU, wallet create, signature) should match a number you can see there.

Cavos ships a [dashboard](https://cavos.xyz/dashboard) where you create an app, copy an App ID, and operate gas sponsorship. The kit reads `NEXT_PUBLIC_CAVOS_APP_ID` in the browser. Published plans include dashboard and usage analytics. The [React integration](/blog/how-to-add-an-embedded-wallet) starts with that App ID.

## Wallet-as-a-service: build versus buy

This is the decision most “embedded wallet platform” searches are actually making.

**Buy a finished SDK** when the wallet is a component of a game, a checkout, or a consumer app. You want hosted login, a wallet handle, sponsored execution, and documented recovery. Cavos, Privy, Dynamic, Magic, and Web3Auth are this purchase. Pick among them on custody, chains, and whether you must connect wallets users already hold.

**Buy infrastructure** when the wallet *is* the product, or when you need server-side signing, quorums, and policy as features you sell. Turnkey is this purchase. You still write auth, approval UI, recovery, and chain adapters. The [Privy vs Turnkey](/blog/privy-vs-turnkey) guide is the worked split.

**Build** only if no vendor ships the chain or account model, the threat model forbids a third-party registry, or a regulator requires you to operate the keys. Building means you own generation, backup, device restore, fee sponsorship, and the lost-phone incident. Most teams that start here later buy one of the two layers above.

Migration is a project in every direction. Addresses are derived differently. Confirm export, import, and what happens if you cancel before you treat any platform as interchangeable.

## Wallet-as-a-service pricing models

**Wallet as a service** invoices do not meter the same thing. A cheap free tier with a tight MAU cap is not the same as a flat wallet-create allowance. Read the unit before the dollar amount. The usual meters are monthly active users, monthly active wallets, wallet creates, signatures, and volume. Published figures below already appear on this site’s [compare](/compare) and [providers](/blog/embedded-wallet-providers) pages, as of October 2026. They can change. This page does not invent unpublished enterprise prices. Worked cost at 10k / 100k / 1M users is [how much embedded wallets cost](/blog/embedded-wallet-cost).

**Monthly active users (MAU).** A user who logs in or uses a wallet during the month counts once. Privy, Dynamic, and Para publish MAU plans. Privy’s public free tier is 0–499 MAU with 50,000 signatures and $1M volume per month; Core is $299/mo and Scale is $499/mo. Dynamic’s Growth plan is $249/mo for up to 5,000 MAUs; extra-MAU is billed, but the public plan card and FAQ disagree on the per-MAU rate, so that rate is omitted here. Para is free to 1,200 MAUs, then $200 / $500 / $1,000 per month.

**Monthly active wallets (MAW).** The same idea, keyed on the wallet. MetaMask Embedded Wallets (formerly Web3Auth) starts free at 1,000 MAWs; Growth is $69/mo and Scale is $399/mo. Magic’s Developer plan includes 1,000 MAWs and 50,000 signatures at $0/mo; the next listed team plan is $99/mo with 2,500 MAWs.

**Per wallet, or wallet creates.** The invoice grows when you provision addresses. [Cavos pricing](/pricing) is free for the first 1,000 wallet creates, then Essential at $59/mo and Complete at $139/mo. Turnkey’s Pay as you go tier includes 1,000 wallets and 25 free signatures per month. Dfns publishes annual and quarterly platform subscriptions with a wallet allowance (Starter 10 wallets, Basic 10,000, Pro 50,000).

**Per signature.** Useful when signing is the scarce resource. Turnkey then charges $0.10 per signature; Pro is $99/mo. Dfns includes a monthly signature allowance on each plan (100 / 1,000 / 10,000 / 100,000) rather than a public per-signature list price. Privy also caps signatures on the free tier, so a chatty app can hit a signature wall before the MAU wall.

**Volume.** Privy’s free tier publishes a $1M volume cap. That meter fits a checkout better than a game that signs often and settles little. Gas sponsorship is often a separate line, or reserved for a higher plan. Ask what is metered, what is rate-limited, and whether the first sponsored transaction is on the public plan.

## Who each wallet-as-a-service provider fits

The providers above serve different product shapes. Write down what you ship before you compare prices.

- **Cavos** fits consumer apps where the user should never see a wallet picker, the chain is Starknet, Solana, or Stellar, and the security review requires that the vendor cannot reconstruct a signing key. Device-native keys, no MPC, paymaster-sponsored execution.
- **Privy** fits EVM-first products that want one vendor for login, an in-app wallet, and connectors for wallets users already hold. TEE-based key infrastructure. Privy is also Alchemy's recommended signer after the Account Kit deprecation (see [Alchemy vs Privy](/blog/alchemy-vs-privy)). Replacement shortlist: [Privy alternatives](/privy-alternatives).
- **Dynamic** fits teams that need connectors as a first-class feature, plus TSS-MPC embedded wallets, plus a long named-chain Tier 1 list (including Stellar, Sui, Aleo, and Midnight). Replacement shortlist: [Dynamic alternatives](/dynamic-alternatives).
- **Turnkey** fits teams building a wallet or custody product that need policy, quorums, and server-side signing primitives. Turnkey is signing infrastructure, not a finished SDK. Replacement shortlist: [Turnkey alternatives](/turnkey-alternatives).
- **Dfns** fits teams that want MPC/TSS infrastructure whose docs say the private key is never reconstructed, including delegated passkey wallets and hybrid deployment models. Dfns publishes annual platform subscriptions, not MAU plans. Replacement shortlist: [Dfns alternatives](/dfns-alternatives).
- **Web3Auth / MetaMask Embedded Wallets** fits teams that want the MetaMask brand, social login, and chain-agnostic MPC (secp256k1 and Ed25519, native EVM and Solana, plus guides for other networks). Replacement shortlist: [Web3Auth alternatives](/web3auth-alternatives).
- **Para** fits teams that want MAU-only pricing (no per-signature charge on public plans) and a named table covering EVM, Solana, Cosmos chains, and Stellar. The public pricing FAQ lists SOC 2 Type II. Replacement shortlist: [Para alternatives](/para-alternatives).

The named-vendor comparison is also on [embedded wallet providers](/blog/embedded-wallet-providers). One-to-one pages are under [compare](/compare).

## Wallet-as-a-service vs embedded wallet vs wallet infrastructure

Searchers type all three. They name the same job from different angles.

- **[Embedded wallet](/embedded-wallet)** is the UX: the wallet lives inside the application, login selects the user, and approval happens in the product UI. There is no seed phrase in the happy path and no second program to install.
- **Wallet-as-a-service (WaaS)** is how that wallet is sold: an API or SDK, a dashboard, and an invoice. Teams also type wallet as a service providers and embedded wallets as a service.
- **Wallet infrastructure** usually means the lower layer: key management, signing primitives, policy, and custody, which a platform assembles into a finished product. Turnkey and Dfns sit closer to this layer. Privy, Dynamic, Magic, Web3Auth, and Cavos ship a finished login-to-signature path.

The [embedded wallet](/embedded-wallet) page is the UX definition. This page is the commercial map. The [Privy vs Turnkey](/blog/privy-vs-turnkey) guide is the build-versus-buy fork between a finished SDK and signing infrastructure.

## Where Cavos fits as a wallet-as-a-service

Cavos is a hardware-isolated, non-custodial [embedded wallet](/embedded-wallet) platform, not an extension, and not a connector. Signing keys are created and used on the user’s device. Keys never leave the device in plaintext. Signing always happens on the device. Cavos cannot see them, sign with them, decrypt them, or move funds. Login never signs a transaction. There is no MPC and no hosted spend key. Auth is hosted Google, Apple, or email, or an identity you pass in. Recovery is a passkey or opt-in AWS Nitro Enclave sealed restore of the MasterDEK; the passkey and the enclave only hold an encrypted copy and cannot sign. Gas Sponsorship in Any Token is on every plan (USDC is one example of a token the user already holds), funded separately.

Adapters ship today for three chains:

- **[Starknet](/embedded-starknet-wallet).** An audited OpenZeppelin account contract (AccountUpgradeable v3.0.0) controlled by one stark key. Cavos does not ship its own account implementation. There are no on-chain device signers and no on-chain social recovery. The paymaster sponsors deploy and execute.
- **[Solana](/embedded-solana-wallet).** A native Ed25519 system account. A relayer co-signs as fee payer.
- **[Stellar](/embedded-stellar-wallet).** A classic G… account. One Ed25519 spend key is HKDF of the MasterDEK. The relayer can sponsor reserves and fee-bumps. A new device restores the MasterDEK and derives the same key.

The React path is `@cavos/kit`. Mobile is `@cavos/kit/react-native`. The current kit release is [Cavos Kit 0.5.0](/blog/cavos-kit-0-5-0). The [React integration](/blog/how-to-add-an-embedded-wallet) and the [quickstart](https://docs.cavos.xyz/docs/quickstart) are the install. [Pricing](/pricing) publishes the free 1,000 wallet creates, Essential at $59/mo, and Complete at $139/mo with enclave recovery.

**Choose Cavos** when the user should never see a wallet picker, the chain is Starknet, Solana, or Stellar, and the review requires that the vendor cannot reconstruct a signing key.

**Choose someone else** when the product is EVM-first, when you must connect MetaMask or WalletConnect, when you need server-side signing or organizational quorums, or when you want one vendor across dozens of EVM networks. Cavos does not inject into `window.ethereum` and does not speak WalletConnect. Those are honest gaps.
